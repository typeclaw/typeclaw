---
title: Watches
description: Probe scheduling, durable verdicts, and delivery of follow-up reminders.
---

`src/watches/service.ts` runs under `typeclaw run`, independently of cron. The first probe is due immediately; subsequent intervals by age are 1 minute before one hour, 5 minutes before one day, 15 minutes before one week, then 30 minutes. A `next` hint is clamped to 1 minute–1 hour. At most two probes are running. Failures back off exponentially; only failure three in each streak queues a diagnostic wake. A quiet verdict, duplicate summary, or human input breaks a wake streak. The fifth consecutive wake pauses the watch. Paused watches still expire.

`store.ts` publishes the verdict, probe state, next due time and pending reminders together by atomic rename to `.typeclaw/watches.json`. Both the authority file and its temporary staging area are masked from model tools. A restart reruns a probe whose verdict never committed. Expiry queues a final wake even if the watch was paused. The verdict and delivery acknowledgment are separate commits: a crash between delivery and acknowledgment can repeat a reminder.

`runner.ts` uses the hook-wrapped bash builtin, not cron exec. It gives the command 120 seconds and reads the last 8 KiB of each output stream. `TYPECLAW_WATCH_ID` identifies the watch; `TYPECLAW_WATCH_LAST_RUN_AT` is the previous completion as an ISO instant, absent initially. The private-mode directory at `workspace/.typeclaw-watches/<id>/` survives between ticks and is removed on cancellation, completion or expiry. This directory is probe scratch space, not trusted authority. Registered bundled probes run in-process with JSON state and a per-repo token resolver.

`delivery.ts` coalesces pending reminders for two seconds. Attachment and idle events retry held TUI delivery. For channels, journal admission does not acknowledge delivery: the watch store keeps the verdict body until the SDK emits `agent_start` and the router records the claim, or the verdict is suppressed. Replays look up the original `watch:<watchId>:<runId>` admission even after an adapter account change. The first account stays bound to that verdict: a replacement account cannot revive a suppressed run, but it can receive a new run. If the process restarts before dispatch, a still-pending verdict requeues against its original admission instead of creating a recovery notice.

The router groups adjacent results for the same requester into one system reminder. After SDK prompt preparation and `before_agent_start`, it checks cancellation, `/stop`, the adapter account and the requester’s current `channel.respond` permission before handing the batch to the agent. If the adapter temporarily cannot identify its account, an unstarted verdict stays pending with its admission open. A known replacement account suppresses that verdict instead. Rejected verdicts never enter agent history; the router rerenders any surviving verdicts. At boot it compares unstarted admissions with the restored watch store: withdrawn verdicts close silently and pending verdicts remain replayable.

That turn claims only its verdicts: a human request waiting for a child stays with its original turn. Reopening a session for a watch leaves the one-time lost-child context for the next ordinary turn. A queued watch is not a recovery opportunity for a human fallback. Human retries finish before fresh watches or another requester’s child completion. Watch retries carry the requester explicitly and recheck `channel.respond` before prompting. Denials, provider failures and empty turns do not post notices. Fresh wakes reuse the cron prompt driver (including fallback, hooks and child drain) with `jobKind: 'watch'`.
